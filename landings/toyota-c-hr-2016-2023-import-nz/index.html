Why Kiwis choose the C-HR

Buyers look at the C-HR because it is easier to park than a larger SUV while still giving five doors and five seats. The ZYX11 hybrid has a listed 25.8 km/L WLTC figure on the G Mode-Nero Safety Plus III, while the NGX50 gives 4WD for buyers who prefer the petrol turbo layout.

What sets it apart03

The clever bits

C-HR equipment changes by body code, year and grade, so these items need to be confirmed on the exact auction car.

Toyota Safety Sense

Toyota's 2022 C-HR equipment table shows Toyota Safety Sense as standard on the grades in that table, with Pre-Collision Safety including daytime and night-time pedestrian detection, daytime cyclist detection, Lane Tracing Assist, Automatic High Beam and all-speed Radar Cruise Control. We confirm whether the exact auction car carries the listed suite before bidding.

Display Audio and DCM from the 2019 minor change

From the 2019 minor change, Display Audio and DCM are listed across the C-HR range. This is a useful dividing line when comparing an earlier car with a later NGX10, NGX50 or ZYX11.

Parking-support equipment on selected grades

Intelligent Clearance Sonar, Parking Support Brake, Panoramic View Monitor and Rear Cross Traffic Auto Brake are listed as optional or special-grade equipment depending on year and grade. Mode-Nero Safety Plus versions are worth checking closely because some of this parking equipment is specifically listed there.

GR Sport grades

S-T GR Sport appears in NGX10 and NGX50 1.2 turbo form, while S GR Sport appears in ZYX11 hybrid form. The badge changes the buying brief, so we confirm the exact grade and drivetrain before any comparison is made.

Fitted technology04

Under the skin

The C-HR is simple to understand once the body code is confirmed, because each requested code points to a different drivetrain path.

01

ZYX11 Toyota Hybrid System II

ZYX11 is the C-HR hybrid code in this search. It uses Toyota’s THS II system with the 2ZR-FXE 1.8-litre engine and an electric continuously variable transmission driving the front wheels. It is a self-charging hybrid, so there is no charging cable or plug-in routine. Around town, the system can move between electric assistance and petrol power without the driver managing modes.

02

NGX10 and NGX50 8NR-FTS turbo

NGX10 and NGX50 use Toyota’s 8NR-FTS 1.2-litre turbo petrol engine, with Toyota's C-HR catalogue data listing output at 85kW. NGX10 is the front-drive version, while NGX50 is the 4WD version with Super CVT-i. After the 2019 minor change, NGX10 petrol grades include both Super CVT-i and 6-speed iMT options depending on grade. This is why the transmission has to be confirmed from the exact auction list before pricing two cars against each other.

03

2016-2023 C-HR size and body layout

The 2016-2023 C-HR import search covers NGX10, NGX50 and ZYX11, with NGX10 added from 2018 and the ZYX11 hybrid appearing after the 2019 minor change. Catalogue examples for post-2019 NGX10 and ZYX11 list a five-door, five-seat body at 4385mm long, 1795mm wide and 1550mm high. NGX50 examples are listed at 1565mm high, which reflects the 4WD packaging. The wheelbase listed for the ZYX11 G Mode-Nero Safety Plus III is 2640mm, so it sits in the compact SUV class rather than the larger family-SUV space.

Honest fit10

Is this car right for you?

A good match if

The C-HR suits someone who wants Toyota hybrid efficiency or a compact SUV with a bit more shape than a boxy hatch. It is not the roomiest Toyota SUV, so buyers carrying bulky prams or tall rear-seat passengers should compare it with Corolla Cross before bidding. For commuting, parking and weekend use, the ZYX11 hybrid is the economy-focused option.

Who this may not suit

  • If you need maximum rear-seat access or a large boot opening, compare Corolla Cross before choosing a C-HR.
  • If you want 4WD and hybrid together, NGX10, NGX50 and ZYX11 do not provide that combination.
  • If you need a confirmed exact arrival date before purchase, importing is not the right process.
