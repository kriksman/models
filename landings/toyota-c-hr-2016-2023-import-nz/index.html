Why Kiwis choose the C-HR

The C-HR appeals because it is easy to place in town, but it still gives you the higher driving position many buyers want. The hybrid version is the one most people ask about, because Toyota's 1.8 petrol-electric setup is familiar, simple to live with, and well suited to commuting. Technical specifications02

C-HR technical specifications

The big draw with the C-HR is that Toyota gave buyers two genuinely different powertrains in the same body. ZYX11 hybrid cars use the 1.8-litre 2ZR-FXE petrol-electric setup, while NGX50 cars use the 1.2-litre 8NR-FTS turbo. That matters because you can shop the same shape around your real use, hybrid for lower day-to-day fuel spend, or turbo AWD grades such as G-T if you want extra traction. One useful detail many buyers miss is that E-Four hybrid availability was part of the model story early on, so drivetrain needs should be checked by model code and grade rather than assuming every hybrid is front-wheel drive.

What sets it apart

The C-HR makes more sense when you focus on the useful equipment differences between grades, not just the shape.

Toyota Safety Sense on later high-spec cars

Later examples and special editions can include Toyota's active safety package, depending on grade and year. That is worth chasing because it bundles the driver-assist features buyers actually use, rather than cosmetic extras alone.

GR SPORT factory trim

GR SPORT is not just a badge to search by. It gives buyers a factory sport-oriented version of the C-HR, which matters if you want the sharper end of the range without piecing a car together from accessories.

Mode-Nero Safety Plus III special editions

Fitted technology04

Under the skin

The C-HR range is worth understanding properly because the model code tells you a lot about how the car will feel to own.

01

ZYX11 1.8 Hybrid

ZYX11 is the code to know if you are searching for the 1.8-litre hybrid version. It uses Toyota's petrol-electric setup based on the 2ZR-FXE 1797cc engine, with 72kW from the petrol engine. Because it is a self-charging hybrid, there is no plug to deal with and no change to how you refuel the car. The appeal is straightforward, lower fuel use in traffic and Toyota hybrid hardware that many NZ buyers already understand.

02

NGX50 1.2 Turbo

NGX50 is the 1.2-litre turbo C-HR, built around the 8NR-FTS 1196cc engine, rated at 85kW. This is the version to look at if you want the turbo powertrain and the AWD-linked grades listed in the requested range. It gives the C-HR a different character from the hybrid, less about electric assistance and more about a small-capacity turbo doing the work. If your shortlist includes G-T or G-T Mode-Nero Safety Plus III, this is the code and engine family behind them.

03

2016-2023 production run

This model run starts in late 2016 and continues through 2023, with early cars and later facelift-era cars both appearing in the auction market. That spread matters because equipment, safety features and special editions changed through the run. If you are trying to avoid overpaying for the wrong spec, year, model code and grade need to be checked together rather than treated as interchangeable.

04

Grade ladder in the requested scope

Confirmed grades in the requested scope include G and G Mode-Nero Safety Plus III on ZYX11 hybrid cars, G-T and G-T Mode-Nero Safety Plus III on NGX50 turbo AWD cars, plus S GR Sport and S-T GR Sport as the factory sport grades. For most buyers chasing low running costs, the hybrid G grades are the natural starting point. If you want the sportier factory look, the GR SPORT grades are the trims to target.

Honest fit10

Who is this car right for?

A good match if

This suits someone who wants a compact SUV for daily use, values Toyota hybrid familiarity, and does not need the cabin space of a RAV4 or Harrier. It also works for buyers who like the idea of AWD but do not want to jump into a much larger vehicle, as long as the exact NGX50 or hybrid drivetrain they want is confirmed before bidding. For that kind of buyer, it is a solid match.

Who this may not suit

  • If you need a large back seat or a bigger boot, a RAV4, Harrier or similar SUV will make more sense.
  • If you want one fixed spec across the whole range, the C-HR needs a bit more homework because drivetrain and grade differences matter.
