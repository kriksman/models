Fitted technology04

Under the skin

The 2023 Prius matters because Toyota changed the hardware, not just the shape.

01

XW60 generation and model range

This is the XW60 Prius, launched in Japan in 2023. The hybrid range started first, then the plug-in version followed in March 2023. In this shape, the line-up matters because there are three distinct powertrain paths: 1.8-litre 2ZR-FXE hybrid, 2.0-litre M20A-FXS hybrid, and 2.0-litre M20A-FXS plug-in hybrid. If you are shopping by auction, that means you need the exact grade confirmed before you assume performance or battery size.

02

2.0-litre M20A-FXS plug-in hybrid system

The PHEV uses Toyota's 2.0-litre M20A-FXS with a 13.6kWh battery and a combined system output of 164kW. In plain terms, it behaves like an EV for shorter battery-backed driving, then works as a hybrid once the battery charge drops or when more sustained power is needed. That is why the car can offer both an 87km WLTC EV figure and the long-trip flexibility people still like about a Prius.

03

Grade ladder and what to look for

For the 2023 shape, the main hybrid grades are X, U, G and Z, while the launch plug-in model was Z PHEV. The 1.8-litre 2ZR-FXE sits in X and U, while G and Z hybrids move to the 2.0-litre M20A-FXS with 196 PS. The best-known grades to target depend on use: U if efficiency matters most, G if you want the 2.0 hybrid without chasing the top trim, Z for the fuller 2.0 hybrid spec, and Z PHEV if plug-in range is the whole point. That last one is the flagship and the one to focus on if you want the full 13.6kWh battery and 87km WLTC EV range.

Honest fit11

Who is this car right for?

A good match if

This suits someone who wants one hatch to cover commuting, errands and open-road trips without stepping into a full EV. It also works for buyers who like Toyota hybrid running costs but want a newer cabin, more power and a proper plug-in battery rather than just a small hybrid pack. If that sounds like you, this is a solid match.

Who this may not suit

  • If you do not plan to charge a plug-in hybrid and just want the lowest purchase cost, a regular Prius hybrid, Aqua or Corolla Hybrid can make more sense.
  • If you need a taller seating position or more rear-seat space, this low-slung hatch is probably not the right shape.
